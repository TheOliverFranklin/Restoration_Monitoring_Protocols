This page provides guidance on how to collect accurate, continuous hydrologic data at wadeable stream sites in support of fish habitat restoration monitoring projects. While much is sourced from RISC manuals, we have adapted the guidance with restoration effectiveness monitoring in mind. As a brief example, we discuss how identifying changes in discharge at a restoration site may require different approaches from characterising watershed flow regimes. We recommend reviewing the Overview section, even if you are very familiar with RISC methods.

Although most looking at this page will want to prioritise quantitative discharge data, there are certain situations where this might be supplemented by or even replaced with data collected in other manners, such as with trailcams. For example, pressure transducers require a certain depth of inundation to collect data, such that under lower flow conditions there may be no data available. Similarly, the information is localised within one point of the channel, and periods of low flow may not be captured if the thalweg shifts. As such, if your restoration actions relate to a suspected case of dewatering or flows that may be too low for fish passage, trailcam-derived data may be appropriate (see Trailcam Stream Connectivity monitoring package, available at this GitHub repository)
